Publication · 2026Lead author

Kompas Pembangunan Masyarakat 2026, Volume 1: Social and Economic Inclusion and Community Capacity Building

A data-driven publication mapping community development across the five sub-districts of the IKN delineation area. Designed as an annual series, with 2026 as the baseline.

Household assetsVocational & job accessEquivalency educationMSMEs & entrepreneurshipDisability & elderly supportHousehold vulnerabilityDigital literacy
Key findings
  • 42.2% of working-age residents (42,246 of 100,120) are neither working nor running a business, and most of them are women (30,750 vs 11,496 men).
  • The gap is shifting from housing to economic capacity. Housing scores highest in household welfare, while 1,848 of 3,122 lowest-tier households have no working-age member at all.
  • Loa Janan is consistently the most disadvantaged, while Sepaku shows a polarised picture as newcomers and local residents live side by side.

Directions in this publication are indicative and are not binding program recommendations.

Policy paper · Oct 2026Author

Prospective Entrepreneurs and Pineapple Downstreaming in Samboja Barat

A full analysis of how processed pineapple can become a business pathway for 137 prospective entrepreneurs from low-welfare households, comparing four program options.

Key findings
  • A small, specific target group. 72 of the 137 live in the two pineapple hubs. 68% are women and 51% have primary education or less.
  • Processing complements fresh sales. It absorbs harvest-peak surplus and second-grade fruit, with jam and filling as the lead candidate.
  • The real gap is the market. A 2018 women farmers' group sold no more than 10% of its processed output.
  • Recommendation: a phased combined approach with group-based incubation at its core, piloted over 24 months.

Analysis material for policy formulation. It does not represent the official position of the Nusantara Capital City Authority.

Policy brief · Oct 2026Author

Developing Prospective Entrepreneurs from Low-Welfare Households through Pineapple Downstreaming

The decision-ready version of the Samboja Barat study: why pineapple, who the program serves, and how to test the market before production starts.

Key findings
  • Why pineapple. Samboja Barat holds 244 of the 298 pineapple farmers in the IKN area (82%).
  • Why not training alone. Without buyers, processed products do not sell.
  • Recommendation: test the market, incubate groups, then formalise licences and capital access, with buyer partnerships from day one.

Analysis material for policy formulation. It does not represent the official position of the Nusantara Capital City Authority.
